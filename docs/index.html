    One cycle, step by step

    This is the order things happen every time you run the bot. The numbers below are a worked example from 2026-09-18; use the live panel above for the latest record.

    1. 1

      Download prices

      Daily closing prices for four ETFs from Tiingo: SPY (US large companies), QQQ (tech), IWM (small companies) and TLT (long-term bonds). Data more than 5 days old is refused, so the bot never trades on old news.

      4,707 trading days, 2008-01-02 to 2026-09-17
    2. 2

      Turn numbers into yes/no facts

      The model can only read true/false facts, such as "SPY's 60-day return is above its 40th percentile." The bot measures 25 things (returns, volatility, trend gaps) and asks 4 questions about each, which gives 100 facts. The cut-offs come from past days only.

      100 true/false facts per day
    3. 3

      Learn from history

      For every past day, the "right answer" is the strategy that earned the most over the next 20 days, after a penalty for bumpy rides. If none clearly wins, the answer is cash. Five Tsetlin Machines, each started from a fixed seed, train on those answers. The last 20 days are left out because their outcome is not known yet.

      5 machines, 1,000 clauses each, trained on 4,587 days through 2026-08-19
    4. 4

      Vote on today

      Every clause that is true today casts a weighted vote for or against a strategy. The five machines' votes are averaged and the top strategy wins.

      winner: cash, +386 votes ahead of momentum, 5 of 5 machines agree
    5. 5

      Turn the strategy into a holding

      Each strategy has a fixed rule for what to hold. Cash means hold nothing.

      target: no position
    6. 6

      Check the risk limits

      The bot only uses 25% of the account at most, and it checks that the account has not fallen too far from its best value.

      drawdown 0.00%, limit not hit
    7. 7

      Trade only the difference

      It compares the target with what you hold, sells first and buys second, and ignores gaps under 0.5% of the account. If any order is still waiting to fill, it skips the whole cycle.

      2026-09-18: SPY sell submitted; the saved record does not confirm a fill

    What the history says the "right answer" usually was

    Across 4,587 labelled days since 2008, cash was the best answer on about seven days out of ten. That is why the model leans toward cash, and a model that leans this way will often say cash.

    Where a Tsetlin Machine fits

    A Tsetlin Machine learns a set of small rules called clauses. Each clause is a few facts joined by AND, for example "SPY is rising AND volatility is not high." When a clause is true today, it casts a vote for a strategy. The strategy with the most votes wins. Because you can read every clause, you can see why it decided.

    What runs today

    The real model works like the toy, only larger. Each Tsetlin Machine has 1,000 clauses for each strategy, learned from 18 years of history, and the bot trains five of them from fixed seeds and averages their votes. Fixed seeds matter: your research code starts each run from a random seed, so the same data could give a different signal every time. With fixed seeds it cannot.

    What this means

    • The Tsetlin Machine did not pick strategies better than a plain equal mix. Neither did the three simpler models. So the problem is not the Tsetlin Machine in particular. Picking strategies did not work on this data.
    • Logistic regression came closest on base settings (Sharpe 0.88 against 0.95), and still came second.
    • The Tsetlin Machine's result swings a lot between variations, from a Sharpe of 0.29 to 0.92. Your research code does not fix its random seed, so repeat runs differ. That is one more reason not to lean on any single number.
    • 2010–2020 was a very strong stretch for US stocks, with SPY gaining about 14.6% a year. That makes any cautious strategy look weak.
    • The locked 2021–2025 period is still untouched. Because the development test failed, your own rules say the right move is not to open it yet.
    What this means for the botToday's "cash" signal has no proven edge. Keep paper trading to test the machinery, and use the shadow signals to collect real-time evidence. Nothing here is a reason to use real money.

    This does not prove that no strategy could work, and it says nothing certain about the future. Your research repo's next experiment (v0.3) asks a different question: can a model reduce risk in bad periods, instead of picking a strategy? That fits this result better.

    Why the test failed, and the plan

    The Tsetlin Machine was trained and it works. All four models failed the same way, which points at the question and the data rather than the machine, but it does not prove it: the labels, the features, the model, or simply too little predictable signal in four price series could each be the cause.

    Where a Tsetlin Machine really helps

    In research it matches simpler models on accuracy; it does not beat them. What it gives you that they cannot is rules a person can read and check. So the plan aims it at a job where that matters: a risk filter you can trust, not a market-beating oracle.

    The safety rules

    First

    Account and drawdown are checked before any data download or training. A broken feed cannot stop the breaker.

    25%

    of the account is the most the bot will put in the market.

    15%

    fall from the account's best value trips the breaker. It sells everything to cash, without needing a model.

    Manual reset

    Once tripped, it stays off until you delete results\state.json. If that file is missing, it refuses to trade.

    4 ETFs only

    Anything else in the account is reported and left alone. The bot cannot sell what it did not buy.

    Sells, then wait

    Buys are placed only after the sells have filled. Every order has an id tied to the run, so a retry cannot double it.

    One at a time

    A lock file stops two runs overlapping. A stale lock from a crash is replaced after 2 hours.

    0.5%

    gaps between target and holdings are ignored, so it does not trade on tiny drift.

    Trading days

    Real runs are skipped on weekends and exchange holidays, using Alpaca's calendar.

    Receipts

    Every decision records the code version, a hash of the price data, package versions and settings. Failures and halts can post to a webhook.

    Can we trade live?

    "Live" can mean two things. Running on live market prices is already happening. Trading with real money is not set up, and the code is written to refuse it: the Alpaca connection is fixed to the paper account.

    My recommendation is to stay on paper for now. The reasons:

    • The bot rebalances about once a week, so a few days of results tell you nothing about whether the strategy works. Give it at least a couple of months.
    • Your research repo says its own backtests are not evidence of profit, and the Tsetlin Machine here has not been shown to beat the simpler baseline.
    • The automatic schedule is configured, but it still needs a successful recorded run and ongoing monitoring.
    • Real-money accounts have their own eligibility rules by country. Check Alpaca's before planning anything.
